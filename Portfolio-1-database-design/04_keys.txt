==========================================================================================
4. PRIMARY KEYS AND FOREIGN KEYS
==========================================================================================

Evidence for Portfolio 1: Database Design and Modelling, Hospital Management System (Miva
Hospital), PostgreSQL.

PRIMARY KEYS (PK)
-----------------

A primary key uniquely identifies each record in a table. It prevents duplicate
identifiers and allows each row to be referenced reliably.

+--------------------+-----------------+---------------------------------------------+
| Table              | Primary key     | Purpose                                     |
+====================+=================+=============================================+
| Patients           | patient_id      | Uniquely identifies each patient.           |
+--------------------+-----------------+---------------------------------------------+
| Doctors            | doctor_id       | Uniquely identifies each doctor.            |
+--------------------+-----------------+---------------------------------------------+
| Appointments       | appointment_id  | Uniquely identifies each appointment.       |
+--------------------+-----------------+---------------------------------------------+
| Prescriptions      | prescription_id | Uniquely identifies each prescription.      |
+--------------------+-----------------+---------------------------------------------+
| Laboratory_Records | lab_id          | Uniquely identifies each laboratory record. |
+--------------------+-----------------+---------------------------------------------+

All primary keys use PostgreSQL's GENERATED ALWAYS AS IDENTITY property, which
automatically generates sequential identification numbers.

FOREIGN KEYS (FK)
-----------------

+--------------------+----------------+------------------------------+
| Table              | Foreign key    | Referenced table and column  |
+====================+================+==============================+
| Appointments       | patient_id     | Patients(patient_id)         |
+--------------------+----------------+------------------------------+
| Appointments       | doctor_id      | Doctors(doctor_id)           |
+--------------------+----------------+------------------------------+
| Prescriptions      | patient_id     | Patients(patient_id)         |
+--------------------+----------------+------------------------------+
| Prescriptions      | doctor_id      | Doctors(doctor_id)           |
+--------------------+----------------+------------------------------+
| Prescriptions      | appointment_id | Appointments(appointment_id) |
+--------------------+----------------+------------------------------+
| Laboratory_Records | patient_id     | Patients(patient_id)         |
+--------------------+----------------+------------------------------+
| Laboratory_Records | doctor_id      | Doctors(doctor_id)           |
+--------------------+----------------+------------------------------+
| Laboratory_Records | appointment_id | Appointments(appointment_id) |
+--------------------+----------------+------------------------------+

Foreign keys establish relationships between tables and maintain referential integrity.
For example, the patient_id foreign key in Appointments ensures that an appointment cannot
reference a patient who does not exist in Patients.

The doctor_id foreign key in Prescriptions identifies the existing doctor responsible for
prescribing the medication.
